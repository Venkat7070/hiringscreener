# Battery Health and Device Care

Battery health is one of the most common questions we receive about refurbished devices. This article explains how we test and guarantee battery health, what battery health percentages actually mean, and how to care for your device's battery over time.

## Our Battery Health Guarantee

Every phone and tablet sold by ReNew Mobile has a battery health of **at least 85% of its original design capacity** at the time of sale. This is tested during our refurbishment process using diagnostic tools appropriate to the platform (Apple's built-in battery health diagnostics for iPhones and iPads, and manufacturer or third-party diagnostic tools for Android devices). Any battery testing below our 85% threshold results in the battery being replaced with a new battery before the device is approved for sale — we never sell a device with a battery below this threshold, regardless of grade or price point.

Some product listings highlight a higher battery health percentage (such as "90%+ Battery Health") when available, for customers who specifically prioritize maximum battery longevity — this is called out on the listing when it applies, but 85%+ is the guaranteed minimum across our entire catalog.

## What "Battery Health Percentage" Actually Means

Every rechargeable lithium-ion battery — in every phone, tablet, and laptop, whether new or refurbished — has a maximum charge capacity that gradually decreases over repeated charge cycles, simply due to the chemistry of how these batteries work. This is normal and expected, not a defect.

"Battery health" is a percentage comparing a battery's current maximum capacity to its original capacity when it was brand new. A battery at 100% health can hold its full original charge; a battery at 85% health can hold about 85% of the charge it could when new. This does not mean the device's battery percentage indicator (the "0-100%" shown in your status bar) is somehow capped or different — a device with 85% battery health still charges to a "100%" indicator on screen, it simply holds less total energy at that 100% mark than it did when the battery was new.

## Why Battery Health Naturally Decreases Over Time

Every charge cycle (one full charge, from roughly empty to full) causes a very small amount of permanent capacity loss in a lithium-ion battery. This is true of every lithium-ion battery in every device ever made, not something specific to refurbished devices. Typical factors that affect how quickly battery health declines over time include:

- Total number of charge cycles accumulated
- Exposure to extreme heat or cold, especially while charging
- Frequently charging to 100% and letting the device sit fully charged
- Frequently letting the device drain to 0% before charging
- Age of the device, independent of usage, since battery chemistry degrades gradually over time even with minimal use

Because of this, it's normal and expected for a device's battery health to gradually decrease during your ownership of it, even after purchasing a device that tested at 85%+ health from us. This gradual decline over the course of normal ownership is not covered under warranty — see the Warranty Policy article for the distinction between this expected behavior and an actual battery hardware defect.

## How to Check Your Device's Battery Health

- **iPhone/iPad:** Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging, which shows "Maximum Capacity" as a percentage.
- **Samsung Galaxy devices:** Settings > Battery and device care > Battery > Battery information (availability of this exact menu path can vary slightly by Android version and Samsung One UI version).
- **Google Pixel and other Android devices:** Battery health information display varies by manufacturer — some models expose this directly in Settings, while others require a manufacturer-specific app or diagnostic code. If you can't locate this on your specific model, our support team can advise based on the model.

## Tips for Preserving Battery Health

While some battery degradation over time is unavoidable, these habits can help slow it down:

- **Avoid extreme temperatures**, especially leaving your device in direct sunlight, a hot car, or very cold conditions, particularly while charging.
- **Avoid letting the battery sit at 0% or 100% for extended periods** — keeping the battery generally between 20% and 80% for day-to-day use, when practical, is gentler on long-term battery health than always charging fully or draining completely.
- **Use the charging cable and adapter appropriate for your device** — using a damaged cable, a very low-quality third-party charger, or a charger not rated correctly for your device's charging speed can generate excess heat during charging, which accelerates battery wear over time.
- **Enable Optimized Battery Charging (iPhone) or equivalent adaptive charging features (many Android devices)**, if available in your device's settings — these features learn your daily charging routine and slow the final stage of charging overnight to reduce time spent at 100%.
- **Avoid using your device while it's charging for extended, demanding tasks** (like gaming) when possible, since this generates additional heat on top of the heat already generated by charging.

## When Battery Replacement Might Be Needed

If your battery health drops significantly and you're noticing reduced battery life, unexpected shutdowns at higher battery percentages, or the device running noticeably hot, this may indicate the battery has degraded to the point where replacement would meaningfully improve your experience. Whether this is covered under warranty depends on how much time has passed since your purchase and how much the decline exceeds what's reasonable for normal use during that period — see the Warranty Policy article for how we evaluate this, or contact support for an individual assessment of your specific situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My new refurbished phone shows 85% battery health right out of the box — is that bad?**
A: No — 85% is our guaranteed minimum standard at time of sale, not a sign of a problem. Many of our devices test higher than 85%, but every device meets or exceeds this threshold before we approve it for sale, and this is disclosed transparently on the product listing.

**Q: Will fast charging damage my battery faster than slow charging?**
A: Modern devices are engineered to manage heat and charging speed safely, including when using fast charging, and manufacturers design their charging systems with long-term battery health in mind. That said, charging generates heat, and heat is one of the factors that affects battery longevity over time — using fast charging occasionally when convenient is fine, but relying on it constantly while also exposing the device to warm conditions is one contributing factor among several that can accelerate wear over time, alongside total charge cycles and age.

**Q: Can I request a device with a brand-new (100% health) battery specifically?**
A: Some listings specifically highlight higher battery health percentages when available in our current inventory — check for these callouts on product listings, or contact support to ask about current availability of higher battery health units for a specific model you're interested in.

**Q: Does using wireless charging degrade the battery faster than wired charging?**
A: Wireless charging is generally slightly less energy-efficient and can generate marginally more heat than wired charging, which is one of several factors that can contribute to battery wear over time, though the difference is generally modest for typical day-to-day use. If maximizing long-term battery health is a top priority, wired charging is a reasonable preference, though this is a minor factor compared to overall charge cycle count and heat exposure.
